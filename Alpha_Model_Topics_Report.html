1. Strategy Overview
The Alpha Model contains two core institutional strategies, each targeting a different market edge.

Monthly Momentum V1 — Top 20 Rotation

MONTHLY REBALANCE • NIFTY 500

A classic cross-sectional momentum strategy that ranks all NIFTY 500 stocks by their 6-month (126-day) return, selects the Top 20, and equally-weights them. Rebalances at the end of each month. Goes 100% cash when the market regime turns bearish.

2. Technical Indicators Used
Institutional indicators and momentum factors powering signal generation.

2.4 — Average True Range (ATR)

Measures daily volatility by computing the true range (max of: High−Low, |High−Prev Close|, |Low−Prev Close|) and averaging over 14 days. Used to set adaptive stop losses and profit targets.

True Range = max(High−Low, |High−Prev Close|, |Low−Prev Close|)
ATR = 14-day Simple Moving Average of True Range
Stop Loss = Entry − 1.5 × ATR  |  Target = Entry + 2.5 × ATR

2.6 — Simple Moving Average (SMA 200 & SMA 50)

Classic trend filters. SMA-200 determines long-term trend direction for individual stocks. SMA-50 is used for market breadth calculations (% of stocks above 50-day MA).

Stock Filter: Close > SMA(200)
Market Breadth: % of Nifty 500 above SMA(50) > 45%

2.7 — 6-Month Momentum Return (V1)

Simple percentage return over 126 trading days. Stocks are ranked by this return to identify the strongest performers for the momentum portfolio.

Momentum = (Close / Close[126 days ago]) − 1

2.8 — 12M-minus-1M Momentum (Alpha Pro)

Uses close price from 21 days ago divided by close from 252 days ago. Skipping the most recent month avoids the well-documented short-term mean reversion effect.

Score = (Close[21d ago] / Close[252d ago]) − 1
This is the Jegadeesh-Titman "12−1" momentum factor.

2.9 — Relative Strength vs Market

126-day return of the stock vs the market proxy. Only stocks outperforming the Nifty 50 are eligible.

Stock Return(126d) > Market Return(126d)

2.10 — Realized Volatility (20-Day Annualized)

Used in Alpha Pro for risk-adjusted momentum scoring and inverse-volatility weighting.

Vol = Std(Daily Returns, 20) × √252
3. Market Regime Filters
Top-down filters that prevent trading during bear markets — the single biggest alpha driver.

3.1 — SMA-200 Regime Filter (Momentum V1)

When the Nifty 50 index trades below its 200-day SMA, the market is declared bearish and ALL trading is halted. 100% cash. This simple rule has historically avoided major drawdowns (2020 COVID crash, 2022 bear market).

Regime = Bullish if NIFTY 50 Close > SMA(NIFTY 50 Close, 200)

3.2 — SMA-100 Fast Regime Filter (Alpha Pro)

Uses a faster 100-day SMA for quicker regime switching. Gets into cash faster during crashes but also re-enters faster during recoveries. Backtested to provide better risk-adjusted returns for the concentrated 5-stock strategy.

Regime = Bullish if NIFTY 50 Close > SMA(NIFTY 50 Close, 100)

3.3 — Absolute Momentum Filter

Requires the Nifty 50's own 126-day return to be positive. Combines with SMA filter for double confirmation during Alpha Pro backtesting.

3.5 — Market Proxy Construction (Backtest)

In backtesting (where Nifty index data may be unavailable for the full history), a synthetic market proxy is constructed by averaging the closing prices of the top 5 large-cap stocks: RELIANCE, HDFCBANK, TCS, INFY, ICICIBANK. The SMA-200 regime is computed on this proxy.

4. Signal Generation & Entry Logic

Momentum V1 & Alpha Pro — Ranking-Based Selection

No complex signal — simply rank, filter, and select:

  1. Filter universe by: Close > SMA(200), Avg Turnover > min threshold, Close > ₹10
  2. Compute momentum score (6M return for V1; 12M−1M for Alpha Pro)
  3. Rank all eligible stocks by momentum score descending
  4. Select Top N (20 for V1, 5 for Alpha Pro)
  5. Alpha Pro only: Apply "Hold Rank Buffer" — keep existing holdings if still within top 15 rank
5. Risk Management & Exit Logic

5.1 — ATR-Based Stop Loss

Stop loss is placed 1.5× ATR below the entry price. Adapts to each stock's volatility — wide stops for volatile stocks, tight stops for calm ones.

Stop = Entry Price − 1.5 × ATR(14)

5.2 — ATR-Based Profit Target

Fixed target at 2.5× ATR above entry. Gives a 1:1.67 Risk-Reward ratio. Ensures winners are systematically bigger than losers.

Target = Entry Price + 2.5 × ATR(14)

5.4 — Maximum Hold Period

Positions are forcibly closed after 60 trading days. Prevents capital from being trapped in sideways moves that didn't hit stop or target.

Exit after 60 trading days

5.4 — Trailing Stop (Alpha Pro)

An optional 15% trailing stop from the peak price, tested during Alpha Pro diagnostics. Activates when price drops 15% from its highest point post-entry.

Stop = Peak Price × (1 − 0.15)
Cooldown = 5 trading days after exit
6. Position Sizing & Portfolio Construction

6.1 — Equal Weighting (Momentum)

V1: 5% per stock (20 stocks). Alpha Pro: 22% per stock (5 stocks × 1.1× leverage). Simple and effective for momentum strategies.

6.2 — Inverse Volatility Weighting

Tested in Alpha Pro diagnostics. Allocates more capital to lower-volatility stocks. Portfolio weight = (1/Vol) / Σ(1/Vol).

6.3 — Sector Capping

Limits each sector to maximum 4 stocks in the portfolio. Prevents concentrated sector bets from dominating returns.

The live simulation engine generates signals at day-close but executes at next-day-open prices — the realistic execution model. This avoids the "close-to-close" bias present in naive backtests.

9. Robustness & Stress Testing
Five independent robustness tests validate that the strategy isn't a statistical artifact.

9.1 — Monte Carlo Simulations (3 Tests)

Test 1: Return Noise Injection (200 sims)

Adds random N(0, 0.5%) noise to each daily return. Simulates real-world execution slippage, bid-ask spread variation, and fill timing uncertainty. Strategy must maintain Median CAGR > 25% and Worst-Case DD > −50%.

Test 2: Trade Shuffle (200 sims)

Keeps the same set of daily returns but randomly shuffles their order. Tests whether performance depends on "lucky sequencing" of trades. If Sharpe survives shuffling, the edge is in trade selection, not timing luck.

Test 3: Crash Injection (100 sims)

Injects a synthetic 5-day, −10%/day crash at a random point in the equity curve. Tests survivability during extreme tail events. Strategy must still show positive CAGR after any crash placement.

9.2 — Liquidity Realism Test

Tests strategy performance under increasingly strict liquidity filters (₹1Cr → ₹10Cr minimum daily turnover). Uses T+1 Open execution and variable slippage based on ADV participation rate. Tracks metrics like average participation, max participation, and % of trades exceeding 5% or 10% ADV.

Pass criteria: CAGR stays > 20% across all liquidity levels, CAGR drop < 30%, DD stays within −50%, Max participation < 20%.

9.3 — 2D Sensitivity Analysis

Grid search over key parameter pairs (e.g., VHF Threshold vs Z-Score Threshold) to verify the strategy isn't sitting on a narrow parameter peak. A robust strategy should show stable performance across a range of nearby parameter values.

9.4 — Isolated Year-by-Year Analysis

Runs the backtest independently for each calendar year to identify whether returns are driven by one or two outlier years or are broadly distributed. Checks for consistency across bull, bear, and flat market environments.

11. Technology Stack & Coding Methodologies
The underlying software engineering and mathematical tools powering the quant model.

11.1 — Python & Vectorized Operations

The entire engine is built in Python. To handle daily data for 500 stocks over 12 years (1.5 million+ data points), the model relies heavily on Pandas and NumPy for vectorized operations, avoiding slow for loops and computing indicators across the entire universe simultaneously.

11.3 — Parquet Columnar Storage

Historical data is stored in the Apache Parquet format (nifty500_daily.parquet) via PyArrow. Parquet provides massive compression (reducing file size to ~19MB) and lightning-fast read speeds compared to standard CSV files, crucial for rapid backtesting iterations.

11.3 — Statistical Standardization (Z-Scores)

The model uses Z-Scores to normalize variables. It calculates rolling means and standard deviations to translate price momentum into statistically significant signals (Z > 1.0).

11.5 — Monte Carlo Simulation Algorithms

The robustness engine uses programmatic simulated randomness (using numpy.random). It applies Gaussian (Normal) distributions to inject noise into daily returns, and uses seeded pseudo-random number generators to identically shuffle trading sequences hundreds of times to isolate the "skill vs luck" factor.
